Crown Shyness

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crown shyness (also canopy disengagement,'' canopy shyness, 2) or inter-crown spacing[3] is a phenomenon observed in some tree species, in which the crowns of fully stocked trees do not touch each other, forming a canopy with channel-like gaps. [45] The phenomenon is most prevalent among trees of the same species, but also occurs between trees of different species. |7] There exist many hypotheses as to why crown shyness is an adaptive behavior, and research suggests that it might inhibit spread of leaf-eating insect larvae.
